## Operator recovery for an unfinished daily scan

The single 02:00 UTC Hobby cron is not a retry mechanism. Until there is an automated continuation path, an operator must inspect the authenticated cron response after the daily run and invoke the same endpoint again with its server-side `CRON_SECRET` if a batch ended with `complete:false`, the request failed, or the queue may contain another race. Wait at least two minutes after an interrupted request so its lease can expire, then repeat. Never put the secret in a browser URL or a client-side bundle. The endpoint is idempotent: a saved cursor resumes, and an existing entry is not charged twice. A 200 response with no batches is insufficient proof that everything is done because another worker may hold a lease.

Verify the queue in the isolated database (and, only after a separately approved activation, in the relevant production database) with this read-only query:

```sql
select e.id, e.name, e.deadline, j.status, j.cursor_team_id,
       j.lease_until, j.processed_count, j.entered_count
from public.events e
left join public.recovery_autopilot_jobs j on j.event_id = e.id
where e.kind = 'one_day' and e.status = 'OPEN'
  and e.scheduled_at is not null
  and e.calendar_source in ('UCI', 'PELOTONIA')
  and e.deadline > now() and e.deadline <= now() + interval '48 hours'
  and (j.event_id is null or j.status = 'PENDING')
order by e.deadline, e.id;
```

Zero rows after all leases expire means there is no currently incomplete eligible race. The query ran against the restored isolated project and returned zero rows. The preview capacity test exercised the manual second invocation and proved completion at the saved cursor. Check again when a new race enters the 48-hour window. If any deadline passes with an incomplete job, do not mark it successful; record the missed race and investigate failed entry attempts. For unattended operation, the product still needs a scheduler or queue that can continue within the deadline when one 35-second invocation is insufficient.

## Frequent-scheduler feasibility probe, 5 October

In the same isolated Supabase project, a temporary `pg_cron` job ran every minute and used `pg_net` to call the protected, branch-only Vercel Preview `/api/cron/autopilot`. The 18:26 and 18:27 UTC runs each returned HTTP 200 JSON with `enabled:true`; no race was due, so both correctly had empty batches and reveals. This proves that a Supabase-hosted scheduler can invoke the authenticated Preview endpoint more often than the Vercel Hobby daily cron. It does **not** prove that a real entry scan or division reveal finishes before its deadline.

The Preview share URL first returned a redirect page to `pg_net`. A short-lived share cookie plus the Preview-only `CRON_SECRET` made the test call work. These are unsuitable as permanent scheduler credentials: the share expires, and an automation bypass configured at Vercel project level may also affect production deployments. The eventual scheduler needs a stable, scoped access method reviewed together with its Vercel protection settings. Store the cron authorization in Supabase Vault, never in the `cron.job` command text. Verify both the SQL job history **and** the asynchronous `net._http_response` status/body; a successful `cron.job_run_details` row only means the HTTP request was queued.

The temporary job was unscheduled, its two Vault secrets deleted, and the `pg_cron`/`pg_net` extensions added for the probe were removed. The isolated database has no remaining scheduler job or test credential. Before activation, repeat the timed race with a failed invocation or scheduler outage. If a job remains incomplete at registration close, the current `recovery_autopilot_claim_job` stops claiming it and reveal cannot proceed; this needs an explicit sporting recovery rule and operator alert rather than a silent late entry or deadline extension.

A follow-up **two-manager timed race** used the same temporary every-minute scheduler and the `scheduler` mode of `tests/support/two-phase-browser-small.mjs`. Both managers registered through the Preview calendar. The real cron route completed their event's entry scan while registration was open; only then did the probe close registration. A later cron call committed the persisted division reveal without a direct reveal call from the probe. Both managers saw their division, saved tactics, watched the recorded race and fetched their results; the replay, awards and repeat preparation checks passed. The fixture event, 16 riders, both disposable manager accounts and teams were removed. The job, Vault secrets and extensions were again removed, with zero fixture events/riders/teams, secrets or temporary extensions on the isolated project. This established the basic timed path. The next probe covers a scan requiring multiple invocations.

A second timed race kept **200 disposable teams** in the isolated project, with two managers completing the browser journey. The first scheduled cron response processed 180 of the 207 total teams and returned `complete:false`; the next processed the remaining 27 from the saved cursor and returned `complete:true`. Registration remained open until the browser probe observed the completed job. After closure, a third scheduled response recorded the division reveal. Both managers then saved tactics, viewed the recorded race and verified their results and unchanged awards on repeat. This proves scheduled continuation across two real Preview function invocations at the measured scale. It still does not test an outage or a missed registration deadline. The fixture event, 16 riders, 200 accounts/teams, cron job, two Vault secrets, extensions and local credential file were removed; guarded queries returned zero remaining test artifacts.

The cron route now returns HTTP 503 with `DIVISION_REVEAL_BLOCKED` and the pending event ID when registration has closed but its autopilot scan is unfinished. A separate service-only read checks open two-phase races after tactics close; if any lacks a saved reveal, the route returns HTTP 503 with `DIVISION_REVEAL_OVERDUE` and the event ID. A scan of more than 100 candidates fails closed rather than reporting health from a truncated set. The local 1,000-team cron fixture verifies a blocked response followed by a successful retry and reveal; a separate overdue fixture verifies the later 503. All seven cron tests, 295 unit tests, ESLint and the production build pass. This is detection, not a recovery policy or an external notification. A monitored scheduler, an operator alert and an explicit missed-deadline rule remain required.
