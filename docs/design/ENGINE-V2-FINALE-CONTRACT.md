## Distance phases

Sporting acceptance must cover two contrasting late-race situations. In a large, organised bunch on a flat finish heading for a sprint, a last-kilometre solo move may be attempted and must be recorded and paid for, but surviving to win should be exceptional when rival sprint teams still have workers, energy and road position. In a contested breakaway with riders from opposing teams, late attacks are expected as part of the fight for the win; the simulation must produce and record at least one genuine attempt when riders can act, including attempts that are contained. A solo rider, exhausted riders and explicit manager hold orders need separate treatment rather than an invented successful split. The exact timing and exception rules remain open. Test both cases with varied group size, route, team orders, chase participation, remaining energy and sprint strength. Compare attempt frequency separately from escape survival and finishing place; do not tune one flat win-rate number or suppress a valid manager order to manufacture the target. The present kilometre motor and isolated short-step candidates do not yet pass this joint sporting acceptance test.

Cross both situations with short/fresh and long/fatigued races. A long race can weaken or remove the nominated chase and lead-out helpers before the finale, so an identical late attack may face a smaller paid response; a short race can leave an organised sprint train intact. Compare matched route, roster and orders while varying distance and accumulated work, and record actual helper road membership, remaining energy and affordable pulls at the attack boundary. Do not infer chase strength from roster nominations alone, and do not grant an escape merely because a distance label says 260 or 300 km: a long race ridden gently can retain helpers, while an aggressively ridden shorter race can lose them. The existing source-linked fatigue probes verify energy carryover, but do not calibrate how often helpers drop or how often attacks survive. In one synthetic ensemble, extra starting fatigue even coincided with *fewer* dropped riders because the paid bunch pace also slowed; fatigue, pace and dropping therefore need joint acceptance tests rather than a forced monotonic rule.

The 5 km boundary changes *which decisions matter*; it is not a universal speed multiplier or a forced bunch sprint. The final 500–1000 m is a sprint phase for a flat, grouped finish, but an uphill, cobbled, narrow-road or solo finish needs different skill emphasis and outcomes. Use the route's recorded finish characteristics, never infer precise turns, width or grade from a generic race label. Until the route contract provides sub-kilometre geometry, the model may use a clearly identified last-kilometre profile fallback for physics, but the viewer must not draw invented bends, lanes or overtakes.

Skills must have traceable effects: sprint and acceleration on a flat launch, positioning and actual lead-out help before and through it, climbing on an uphill finish, surface handling where a surface segment is specified, and fatigue/remaining energy across every finish type. A helper's lead-out costs that helper energy and can benefit the protected rider only while they share a plausible road group. Position should be earned or lost through recorded work and constraints; no rider gets a free passing bonus solely from a high sprint stat. Precommitted orders determine the attempt and timing; managers make no live decisions during replay.

## Lead-out as a team role

Add an explicit precommitted finale plan: ride for a selected finisher, nominate an optional lead-out rider from the same locked lineup, and choose a broad launch intent (early, standard or late). A simple preset should choose a sensible available helper automatically; an expert setting can name the rider. The named rider must be distinct from the finisher and available for that role. If they have been dropped, are in another road group, or have exhausted themselves, the recording shows the plan failing or falling back to another eligible helper. Do not teleport a rider into position or silently replace the finisher. This requires a new locked-order and recording version; the existing v2 `helperIds` and `protect` sprint-train chase factor are not a real lead-out designation.

Initially derive lead-out quality from a *combination* of existing abilities and race state: sustained flat or terrain-specific speed to hold the wheel, positioning/handling to bring the finisher forward, acceleration to raise pace at the chosen launch, endurance and remaining energy to complete the pull, plus current group placement. Leadership may improve coordination, but should not create speed by itself. Sprint skill can help a lead-out rider's final burst but need not be their defining trait. Compare this derived role with a dedicated lead-out stat only if playtests show that existing skills cannot distinguish valuable specialists. Avoid a new persistent skill or migration before that evidence exists.

An isolated `v2-finale-lead-out-candidate-1` selector now checks a proposed finisher and optional nominated worker against eight locked riders and their current road groups and energy. An unavailable nominee produces an explicit reason and may fall back to an eligible nominated helper; it never moves riders between groups. Its score combines route-sensitive riding ability, positioning, acceleration, endurance, strength and remaining energy, and it reports a work-cost rate. This is preparation for a recorded pull, not that pull itself: no energy is spent, no position or speed is granted to the finisher, no player order is stored, and v79 results and points are unchanged. The selected worker must still perform finite work in a future short-step recording before any benefit can be credited.

The separate `v2-finale-lead-out-pull-1` laboratory recording now consumes that selection over the known last-kilometre grid. An early, standard or late intent begins at 1000, 750 or 500 m remaining and aims to end at 300 m. Each frame names the actual worker, finisher, road group, source kilometre, distance and energy spent. If the worker runs out of energy, the trace stops at the affordable distance; the validator recomputes the trace and rejects altered work. The rate is uncalibrated, the group's speed and position do not change, and no rival train or later sprint is simulated. This trace cannot be attached to the official race or point path until its work produces a justified group-position effect in a versioned multi-rider finale.

A read-only bridge can now source this work trace from a validated v2 race exactly one kilometre before the line. It maps each rider in the selected real lineup to their recorded energy and peloton, dropped or named road-group membership, and carries a residual-gap warning if the source has the known chase anomaly. A 40 km recorded scenario verifies the bridge without changing that race's official final kilometre. It does not prove that a train survives the next kilometre's rival attacks or that its finisher stays on the wheel; those require the future group-aware finish simulation.

The pull has a finite duration and an energy/position cost to its worker; it gives a bounded, conditional benefit to the finisher's position and launch, rather than adding free seconds to the final result. It can be disrupted by competing trains, an earlier attack, narrow or difficult finishing terrain, or the finisher losing the wheel. On a steep or solo finish, the same plan may offer little or no benefit. A rider cannot simultaneously do hard bunch work, chase, shelter another captain and perform an uncompensated lead-out in the same distance step. Record worker identity, target identity, start/end distance, actual work, energy cost, position change, interruption and any fallback so results and later commentary use the same facts.

## One outcome, one replay

Version the route, locked orders, tuning, seed and recording schema together. Each final-phase frame needs a monotonic distance/time, rider and road-group identities, relative position or ordering at the resolution the model actually supports, gaps, energy and the decision/event that changed the situation. Result times and rankings are calculated from those same frames. The validator must reject impossible reverse order, unrecorded catches or passes, non-monotonic timestamps, and a final frame that disagrees with the classified finish. Private playback may omit rivals' locked orders or hidden energy while preserving public racing events.

A later viewer can slow the final 5 km, then focus on the last kilometre and final metres, with simultaneous commentary, groups, gaps and position. It may interpolate *between* saved states for smooth motion, but cannot create a sporting event between frames. A solo finish, small-group sprint and full-bunch sprint should have different pacing and commentary, sourced from the recorded outcome. Viewer production follows the motor and recording contract, not the other way around.

## Acceptance before replacing the current finish

1. Fix the known residual-gap and final-kilometre-attack interactions together; a tiny artificial gap must neither preserve an otherwise caught move nor allow an unearned launch to gain many seconds.
