# V2 finale: sporting and recording contract

Status: proposed implementation boundary for point 04, not a verified engine rule. The current `v2-prototype-79` resolves the road in kilometre steps and applies one finishing burst after the last step. This cannot express the order of moves, catches and position changes in the final few hundred metres. The existing race path and saved recordings stay unchanged until a new version passes scenario and replay validation.

The read-only `v2-finale-snapshot-1` handoff now accepts a fully validated kilometre recording at exactly five kilometres to go and carries every actual road group, gap, team and rider ID, remaining rider energy, peloton membership and dropped-rider status into a compact state. A test starts from two independently ordered attackers sharing a recorded group and rejects a forged source energy. This does not move a rider, choose a puller, calculate sub-kilometre pace or rank a finisher. It is the input boundary for a future group-aware finale; the official v2 finish, replay and point contract remain unchanged.

The isolated `v2-finale-group-step-1` now advances one distance slice for a single road group and the bunch. It names one pulling rider in each group, charges every other rider for sheltered travel, stops exactly at an earned catch and carries all riders into the merged group. An honest bridge derives the pullers' experimental speeds and each rider's energy cost from a validated five-kilometre snapshot, locked roster and orders, source route and recorded energy; it rejects a front puller ordered to sit on or a bunch helper from a team that was not engaged in the chase. A test uses two independent attacking teams in one front group and a third team's bunch chase. The recording validator recomputes the slice and rejects an altered energy result. This is one counterfactual step, not an eleven-slice race: it has no group cooperation policy, subsequent merged-group movement, overtaking, sprint ranking or point settlement. Worker pace conversion remains uncalibrated, and source residual-gap warnings still apply.

The separate `v2-finale-group-run-1` laboratory now carries that two-group contest through successive 1 km, 250 m and 100 m slices, keeping the same explicitly named puller in each group. Each slice recomputes their pace from the current energy, charges all riders for the distance actually ridden, records the updated gap and stops at a catch or the line. Validation reproduces the complete run from its input and rejects changed energy. A source-linked 40 km example traverses all eleven slices without a catch; an altered, deliberately synthetic one-second-gap example with a weaker front puller and stronger pursuer catches before the line. These are directional accounting tests, not sporting calibration: the source-linked example carries a `residual_gap_after_sufficient_chase` warning and ends with a very large gap. The run refuses unpaid work from an exhausted follower. It still handles only one road group against the bunch, has fixed pullers, and stops at a catch rather than resolving the merged group's remaining distance. It cannot yet determine a winner, replay a complete finale or award points.

The versioned `v2-finale-group-to-line-1` continuation now resumes that caught group from the precise catch metre, with the nominated bunch worker still pulling and every caught rider paying sheltered travel cost through the remaining slice and subsequent slices. The recording retains the catch frame, ordered merged frames, final energy for every rider and bunch travel time from the five-kilometre boundary; validation recomputes all of it. A synthetic fast-chase case reaches the line after a mid-slice catch without double-counting distance or energy, while the source-linked surviving case retains separate groups. This is still an accounting bridge, not an accepted finale: the source gap can carry the known residual anomaly, speed conversion is uncalibrated, merged-group positions and rider-specific sprint launches do not exist, and no provisional result, replay or point entry changes.

The read-only group run and caught-group continuation are now versioned `v2-finale-group-run-2` and `v2-finale-group-to-line-2`. Their recordings explicitly distinguish `sourceTuningVersion` (the kilometre race supplying the five-kilometre state) from `workerTuningVersion` (the v79 constants used by this experimental short-step pace and energy conversion). A v91 source therefore cannot be mistaken for a v91 finale calculation. This provenance change does not calibrate the conversion or alter any official result, replay or points.

The separate rotation variants `v2-finale-group-run-3` and `v2-finale-group-to-line-3` accept one explicitly named bunch puller per distance slice. Every rotating helper must belong to the actual bunch, be nominated in their locked lineup, and come from a rival team already engaged in the recorded chase at five kilometres to go. The current puller's speed sets the group pace and costs them full work; off-duty helpers still pay for sheltered travel. If the group is caught, the planned puller for each remaining slice continues the merged bunch to the line. A three-team recorded source and a deliberately smaller-gap counterfactual verify alternating paid work through a catch and reject invented helpers or altered frames. The rotation is an experimental supplied schedule, not a tactic inferred from managers' existing orders; it provides no automatic cooperation bonus, group position, sprint placing, or points. Its pace and source-gap limitations remain the same as the fixed-puller variant.

The opt-in `v2-finale-group-run-4` / `v2-finale-group-to-line-4` recording identifies `v2-finale-worker-passive-slope-1` as its pace conversion. It converts each ability point directly with the existing kilometre model's 0.07 seconds per kilometre passive-drift slope and gives the chasing worker the existing two-point bunch draft allowance. The earlier laboratory conversion was roughly 0.27 seconds per point on a 90-second flat kilometre, so this is a material, versioned change in relative speed; the old recordings remain reproducible. A real three-team source verifies that the two variants produce different gaps, and an altered small-gap case verifies a caught rotating group continuing to the line. Matching one slope does **not** calibrate the full race: the source can still have the residual-gap defect, the kilometre motor also includes separate capped drift and chase-capacity recovery, and the short-step runner still lacks group cooperation, positions, launches, and classification. No official finish or point path uses this variant.

The isolated `v2-finale-group-run-5` / `v2-finale-group-to-line-5` variant adds an explicitly supplied front puller for each of the eleven distance slices. Every puller must be in the validated five-kilometre source group, start with work energy, and have a locked order that permits pulling at that slice; the first puller must match the nominated source puller. The current worker alone sets that slice's front pace and pays its full work cost; companions pay sheltered travel cost. A recorded three-team case rotates two independent attacking teams and shows the first worker retaining more energy while the second spends more than in a fixed-worker control. The validator reproduces the trace and rejects an altered rotation. This still does not infer a cooperation schedule from manager orders, handle multiple front groups, position riders within a group, or calculate sprint placings. The variant requires the opt-in passive-slope conversion, whose absolute pace remains uncalibrated, and does not change official results or points.

The first code boundary is a pure `finaleDistanceGrid` schedule: four 1 km finale slices, two 250 m approach slices and five 100 m finish slices. Each slice names its source kilometre and explicitly marks the profile resolution as 1000 m. It does not calculate a rider move, imply precise route geometry, change a result, or enter a saved replay. The motor and recording contract must be extended together before these slices have sporting effect.

An isolated `advanceFinaleGap` step now calculates the relative travel time of a front group and its chaser over one slice. It reports a catch at the calculated distance within that slice rather than preserving a positive residual after the chaser has covered the lead. Work cost scales by the slice's actual length. This is a kinematic unit, not a calibrated race model: the current engine does not provide its speeds, choose workers, spend their energy, merge groups after a catch, or record these steps. The unchanged v79 result remains the only current simulated result.

A separate `v2-finale-pair-2` laboratory runner now connects all eleven slices for one front worker and one pursuing worker. It records distance, both workers' elapsed times, their gap and remaining energy after each slice, stops at a calculated catch, charges only the distance actually ridden in that catch slice, and rejects a plan whose work would spend more energy than available. Validation recomputes the trace from the complete input plan. An isolated worker planner can now derive those speeds and work rates from a rider in an eight-rider lineup, the source-kilometre route profile, current energy and a normalized v2 order. It checks helper nomination and chase permission. The conversion from ability to seconds per kilometre is **experimental and uncalibrated**; the planner assumes the caller supplied authentic locked orders and does not choose a worker from the road group. It does not resolve group merges after a catch, sprint ranking, points, or private playback. This is a bounded proof of player-linked distance, time and energy accounting, not a candidate result for a real division.

The offline `engine-v2-finale-pair-scenarios.mjs` probe holds a four-second starting gap on fictional 300 km routes and imposes arrival energy with 5 km remaining; it does not simulate the first 295 km. With otherwise equal 75-skill riders and orders, a 35-energy leader survives by 4.14 seconds, while the same leader starting at 12 energy is caught. The same climbing specialist and flatter chaser produce a catch on the flat course but a 4.87-second surviving gap uphill. These directional differences are useful regression signals, not calibrated sporting acceptance: one rider chasing alone cannot stand in for a coordinated bunch or rival teams, and the model does not yet resolve an actual sprint.

The read-only `probeFinalePairFromTour` bridge can now take a validated kilometre recording at exactly 5 km to go. It accepts only one solo rider in the sole road group and a nominated helper from a different team already engaged in the bunch chase. It uses the recording's committed riders and orders, route, measured group gap and individual remaining energies. A real 40 km v2 recording exercises this path in the unit suite, including rejection of a non-chasing rival. This is a counterfactual probe of the final 5 km, not a continuation attached to the official frames: the existing kilometre engine still simulates those five kilometres and owns its result. Its known residual-gap and automatic final-attack defects can already affect the source recording; the bridge must not be used to settle that recording or award points.

An isolated `v2-finale-relay-1` probe now alternates two named chase helpers according to an explicit eleven-slice rotation. Only the current puller's calculated speed sets the chase pace; both helpers pay energy on every ridden slice, with the non-puller paying a smaller sheltered cost. Their individual energy, the actual puller, gap, distance and elapsed times are recorded, and validation recomputes the complete trace. A second read-only bridge tests this against a validated three-team v2 recording where both rival teams were already engaged at 5 km to go. This does **not** infer a manager's rotation order from current tactics, model a whole peloton or grant a drafting speed bonus. It stops at a catch and does not rank finishers. A genuine multi-group finale still needs group position, membership, cooperation and post-catch state transitions before it can replace the kilometre finish.

A 300 km unit scenario now feeds the pair probe with energy actually earned and spent over the first 295 km, rather than imposing an arrival value. Its late solo rider reaches the boundary with about 26 energy. The current kilometre model leaves a 0.84-second lead despite a positive chase capacity large enough to cover the previous gap plus passive drift. Both read-only bridges attach `residual_gap_after_sufficient_chase` to this source and similarly affected 40 km sources. Their counterfactual outcome remains computed for diagnosis, but must not be cited as a valid sporting finish while that source warning is present. A very early 300 km solo move can also arrive with zero reserve; the strict worker planner then refuses to invent unpaid finale work. Both cases need a coherent exhaustion and chase-recovery rule before this model can settle a long race.

A paired trial across fictional flat, rolling and mountain routes exposed a coupled finale failure. Subtracting full chase recovery from the passive gap change removes the artificial residual, but makes automatic final-kilometre attacks win two thirds of sampled races on every route. Also suppressing unnamed automatic attacks in the final 5 km nearly eliminates breakaway wins. Both trial changes were reverted. The deterministic balance smoke test now rejects those two degenerate outcome patterns without treating the current v79 balance as approved; a replacement needs a joint road-group, chase and finale rule.

The ensemble now attributes each breakaway winner to a rider already ahead before the final kilometre or one who joined on that kilometre, and reports whether the race had a late residual-gap anomaly. In 10 paired seeds per strategy and category, averaged across those six cells per route, 70% of flat races, 73% of rolling races and 40% of mountain races had a pre-final breakaway winner. A late residual-gap anomaly coincided with a breakaway win in 70%, 70% and 43% respectively. Coincidence does not prove that every such win is false, but it prevents the current break-win rate from serving as a target for a corrected chase model. A further trial capped new final-kilometre gaps at one second while using net chase recovery; automatic final attacks still won two thirds of races, so that trial was also reverted.

The read-only finale bridges and paired ensemble now share one residual-gap audit. It only flags a positive gap in the same continuing rear road group, with no fresh attack and enough recorded chase capacity to cover its previous gap plus passive drift. This prevents a group replacement from being mistaken for an impossible surviving gap. The shared check preserves the measured flat, rolling and mountain anomaly rates; it changes no race frames, orders or provisional results.

## Distance phases

| Distance remaining | Sporting decision | Recording requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More than 5 km | Existing road-group simulation, with fatigue and earlier tactical position carried forward. | Kilometre frames remain the source of truth. |
| 5 km to 1 km | Finale state: remaining helpers, group placement, lead-out work, selective attacks and chase, and the cost of improving position. A break can still win if its real lead and relative speed suffice. | Explicit transitions, workers, group membership, energy, gaps and the cause of every catch/split. |
| Last 1 km | Shorter distance steps resolve timing and position before the finishing effort. Initial target is 250 m steps, subject to route-data and performance tests. | Ordered sub-kilometre frames using distance remaining in metres and elapsed race time. |
| Last 500 m | Sprint or final acceleration uses still-finite energy and the actual road situation. Initial target is 100 m steps; a final tie-break is allowed only within a documented timing resolution. | Recorded launches, passing, catches, separations and line crossing must agree with the result. |

The 5 km boundary changes *which decisions matter*; it is not a universal speed multiplier or a forced bunch sprint. The final 500–1000 m is a sprint phase for a flat, grouped finish, but an uphill, cobbled, narrow-road or solo finish needs different skill emphasis and outcomes. Use the route's recorded finish characteristics, never infer precise turns, width or grade from a generic race label. Until the route contract provides sub-kilometre geometry, the model may use a clearly identified last-kilometre profile fallback for physics, but the viewer must not draw invented bends, lanes or overtakes.

Skills must have traceable effects: sprint and acceleration on a flat launch, positioning and actual lead-out help before and through it, climbing on an uphill finish, surface handling where a surface segment is specified, and fatigue/remaining energy across every finish type. A helper's lead-out costs that helper energy and can benefit the protected rider only while they share a plausible road group. Position should be earned or lost through recorded work and constraints; no rider gets a free passing bonus solely from a high sprint stat. Precommitted orders determine the attempt and timing; managers make no live decisions during replay.

## Lead-out as a team role

Add an explicit precommitted finale plan: ride for a selected finisher, nominate an optional lead-out rider from the same locked lineup, and choose a broad launch intent (early, standard or late). A simple preset should choose a sensible available helper automatically; an expert setting can name the rider. The named rider must be distinct from the finisher and available for that role. If they have been dropped, are in another road group, or have exhausted themselves, the recording shows the plan failing or falling back to another eligible helper. Do not teleport a rider into position or silently replace the finisher. This requires a new locked-order and recording version; the existing v2 `helperIds` and `protect` sprint-train chase factor are not a real lead-out designation.

Initially derive lead-out quality from a *combination* of existing abilities and race state: sustained flat or terrain-specific speed to hold the wheel, positioning/handling to bring the finisher forward, acceleration to raise pace at the chosen launch, endurance and remaining energy to complete the pull, plus current group placement. Leadership may improve coordination, but should not create speed by itself. Sprint skill can help a lead-out rider's final burst but need not be their defining trait. Compare this derived role with a dedicated lead-out stat only if playtests show that existing skills cannot distinguish valuable specialists. Avoid a new persistent skill or migration before that evidence exists.

An isolated `v2-finale-lead-out-candidate-1` selector now checks a proposed finisher and optional nominated worker against eight locked riders and their current road groups and energy. An unavailable nominee produces an explicit reason and may fall back to an eligible nominated helper; it never moves riders between groups. Its score combines route-sensitive riding ability, positioning, acceleration, endurance, strength and remaining energy, and it reports a work-cost rate. This is preparation for a recorded pull, not that pull itself: no energy is spent, no position or speed is granted to the finisher, no player order is stored, and v79 results and points are unchanged. The selected worker must still perform finite work in a future short-step recording before any benefit can be credited.

The separate `v2-finale-lead-out-pull-1` laboratory recording now consumes that selection over the known last-kilometre grid. An early, standard or late intent begins at 1000, 750 or 500 m remaining and aims to end at 300 m. Each frame names the actual worker, finisher, road group, source kilometre, distance and energy spent. If the worker runs out of energy, the trace stops at the affordable distance; the validator recomputes the trace and rejects altered work. The rate is uncalibrated, the group's speed and position do not change, and no rival train or later sprint is simulated. This trace cannot be attached to the official race or point path until its work produces a justified group-position effect in a versioned multi-rider finale.

A read-only bridge can now source this work trace from a validated v2 race exactly one kilometre before the line. It maps each rider in the selected real lineup to their recorded energy and peloton, dropped or named road-group membership, and carries a residual-gap warning if the source has the known chase anomaly. A 40 km recorded scenario verifies the bridge without changing that race's official final kilometre. It does not prove that a train survives the next kilometre's rival attacks or that its finisher stays on the wheel; those require the future group-aware finish simulation.

The pull has a finite duration and an energy/position cost to its worker; it gives a bounded, conditional benefit to the finisher's position and launch, rather than adding free seconds to the final result. It can be disrupted by competing trains, an earlier attack, narrow or difficult finishing terrain, or the finisher losing the wheel. On a steep or solo finish, the same plan may offer little or no benefit. A rider cannot simultaneously do hard bunch work, chase, shelter another captain and perform an uncompensated lead-out in the same distance step. Record worker identity, target identity, start/end distance, actual work, energy cost, position change, interruption and any fallback so results and later commentary use the same facts.

## One outcome, one replay

Version the route, locked orders, tuning, seed and recording schema together. Each final-phase frame needs a monotonic distance/time, rider and road-group identities, relative position or ordering at the resolution the model actually supports, gaps, energy and the decision/event that changed the situation. Result times and rankings are calculated from those same frames. The validator must reject impossible reverse order, unrecorded catches or passes, non-monotonic timestamps, and a final frame that disagrees with the classified finish. Private playback may omit rivals' locked orders or hidden energy while preserving public racing events.

A later viewer can slow the final 5 km, then focus on the last kilometre and final metres, with simultaneous commentary, groups, gaps and position. It may interpolate *between* saved states for smooth motion, but cannot create a sporting event between frames. A solo finish, small-group sprint and full-bunch sprint should have different pacing and commentary, sourced from the recorded outcome. Viewer production follows the motor and recording contract, not the other way around.

## Acceptance before replacing the current finish

1. Fix the known residual-gap and final-kilometre-attack interactions together; a tiny artificial gap must neither preserve an otherwise caught move nor allow an unearned launch to gain many seconds.
2. Paired seeds show a catchable late move caught by coordinated sprint teams, a strong break surviving insufficient chase, and a well-timed solo attack succeeding sometimes without dominating every finish.
3. Flat, uphill and specified-surface finishes reward different rider strengths; conserving energy and arriving well placed matter, especially after 240–300 km, without making one generic order optimal.
4. Check two-team and larger fields, both men's and women's races, seeded determinism, finite helper work, separated groups, photo finishes, and exact agreement of recording, result and point inputs.
5. Measure the added frame count, storage size and execution time in the isolated 45-team, three-division flow before enabling the new recording version in any preview player path.
6. Paired lead-out scenarios compare a suitable specialist, a strong sprinter with poor positioning/endurance, an exhausted nominated helper, rival trains and an uphill finish. A successful plan improves a plausible finisher's chance but never guarantees victory or overcomes a surviving road gap without a recorded catch. The viewer can identify who actually led out whom from the saved frames.

Switching a team to `sit_on` or `ignore` removes its worker, and a selective team still waiting to engage at the source cannot be inserted as a chaser.
